Artificial intelligence-driven surrogate modeling for computationally efficient and digitally decarbonized LNG process optimization under varying feed composition

  • Mahmoud Al-Mughairi
  • , Ahmad Saeed
  • , Ashfaq Ahmad
  • , Muhammad Abdul Qyyum*
    • *Corresponding author for this work
  • Petroleum and Chemical Engineering

Research output: Contribution to journal › Article › peer-review

  • Overview

Abstract

Liquefied natural gas (LNG) production remains a energy-intensive process whose optimization presents significant computational challenges. Despite extensive research, existing optimization techniques rely on full-order simulations that are slow, energy-intensive, and unsuitable for rapid decision-making under varying natural gas composition. This study proposes an AI-driven surrogate modeling and optimization framework for designing LNG processes in small-scale and offshore applications, particularly under varying feed gas compositions. Random Forest-based surrogate models, trained on systematically generated simulation data, are used to predict key performance indicators, including specific energy consumption (SEC), minimum internal temperature approach (MITA), and heat-exchanger cost metric (UA), and are integrated with multiple metaheuristic optimizers. Results demonstrate that the AI-driven optimization achieves high predictive accuracy, with R2 values of 0.95 for SEC, 0.98 for MITA, and 0.93 for UA, while reducing optimization time and computational energy by over 90% compared to conventional optimization techniques. Although not yet integrated into control environments, the framework demonstrates strong potential for coupling with distributed control systems to accelerate project delivery. The findings highlight that the potential of AI-driven surrogate models to accelerate LNG process design, reduce digital carbon footprint, and enable faster decision-making in next-generation, energy-efficient, and cost-effective liquefaction systems.
